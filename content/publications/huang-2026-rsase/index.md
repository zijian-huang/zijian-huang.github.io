abstract: Protection measures are widely implemented to enhance the resilience of coastal ecosystems. However, the long-term, satellite-observable mechanisms by which it protection influences mangrove response to typhoon disturbances remain insufficiently explored. Using the MODIS NIRv time series (1999–2024) across coastal China, we conducted a decadal-scale remote sensing analysis to quantify long-term baseline productivity, typhoon-induced damage, and recovery trajectories, in protected and non-protected mangrove patches. Our results reveal a clear protection-driven resistance-resilience trade-off: although mangrove canopies within protected areas maintained significantly higher long-term productivity and suffered greater canopy damage during typhoon events (low resistance), they recovered substantially faster after the storms (high resilience). This trade-off is mechanistically linked to the elevated baseline productivity fostered by long-term protection. This study provides empirical evidence that conservation protection enhances mangrove resilience at decadal scale by promoting environment-adaptation processes detectable from space. Our findings highlight the vital role of long-term satellite-based monitoring in objectively evaluating conservation effectiveness and guiding adaptive mangrove management strategies under increasing climate disturbances.
